## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The analysis demonstrates that traffic collisions in New York City represent a systemic public health challenge heavily concentrated by geography, corridor type, and vehicle class. **While total crash volume has dropped significantly since 2018, the severity and lethality of individual collisions have escalated substantially.** Overwhelmingly driven by preventable human behaviors – **primarily driver distraction, tailgating, and failure to yield** – collisions pose the highest fatal risk to vulnerable road users like pedestrians and cyclists, who lack physical frame protection against increasingly large passenger SUVs and commercial trucks. Addressing this crisis requires shifting from passive driver messaging to aggressive, forgiving infrastructure redesigns and targeted policy interventions. 

<br>

**Key Metrics Highlights**
  * **Citywide Collision Scale:** The analysis documents a total of **1,048,575 motor vehicle collisions** across New York City. 
  * **Human Casualties:** Crashes resulted in **391,816 total casualties**, comprising **389,910 non-fatal injuries** and **1,906 deaths.** Nearly 1 out of every 2.7 collisions results in human injury or death.
  * **Severity Rates:** Approximately **37.18% of crashes result in injury (371.85 per 1,000 collisions)**, while **0.18% result in fatalities (1.82 per 1,000 collisions).**
  * **Vulnerable Road Users (VRUs):** A total of **102,100 pedestrians and cyclists were injured or killed.** **Pedestrians account for 64.5% of VRU casualties (65,849)**, while **cyclists account for 35.5% (36,251).**
  * **Geographic Distribution:** **Brooklyn** leads all boroughs in crash volume with **308,791 crashes (29.45% of city total)**, followed by **Queens** with **282,847 crashes (26.97%).**
  * **Temporal Peak:** **Midday hours**, specifically 12:00 PM, **represent the primary crash time window across the city.**

<br>

**Collision Trends Highlights**
* **Multi-Year Decline in Volume:** Total annual collisions dropped **58% from a peak of 231,563 in 2018 down to 96,570 in 2023.** This decline was driven initially by **COVID-19 traffic reductions in 2020 (112,916 crashes)** and **sustained by Vision Zero traffic-calming initiatives.**
* **Borough Spatial Concentration:** **Brooklyn** and **Queens** combined account for **over half (56.4%) of all collisions in NYC.** **Staten Island** recorded the **lowest share at 4.42% (46,374 crashes).**
* **High-Collision Corridors:** Surface arterials such as **Broadway (16,928 crashes)** and **3rd Avenue (14,243 crashes)** join expressways like the **Belt Parkway (15,463 crashes)** as the **city's highest-volume crash corridors.**
* **Top Danger Hotspots:** The **intersection of Flatbush Avenue Extension** and **Tillary Street** in Brooklyn represents **the most dangerous single hub in NYC**, recording nearly 500 collisions across directional logs.

<br>

**Contributing Factors Highlights**
* **Dominance of Human Error:** **Over half (52%)** of all crashes with recorded causes **are driven by three driver behaviors**: **Driver Inattention/Distraction (260,572 crashes; 32.6%)**, **Following Too Closely (85,473 crashes; 10.7%)**, and **Failure to Yield Right-of-Way (72,367 crashes; 9.0%)**.
* **Secondary Causes:** **Improper passing/lane usage (46,146)** and **unsafe backing maneuvers (42,472)** round out the top 5 primary crash causes.
* **Borough Behavioral Disparities:** Unsafe Speed ranks among the top 10 crash causes in outer boroughs (Queens #7, Staten Island #6, Bronx #9, Brooklyn #10), whereas traffic gridlock keeps speeding out of Manhattan's top 10 list.
* **Reporting Artifacts:** Specific distraction sub-categories like **texting (41)** or **headphone use (29)** reflect low numbers because investigating officers routinely default to the catch-all category Driver Inattention/Distraction on field reports.

<br>

**Injury and Fatality Highlights**
* **Borough Casualty Concentrations:** **Brooklyn (121,704 injured, 525 killed)** and **Queens (105,523 injured, 526 killed)** suffer **63% of all traffic casualties** in New York City.
* **Mode Exposure Disparity:** While **motorists account for 73.5% of total injuries (280,951)**, **pedestrians account for 46.8% of total deaths (871 fatalities)**. A pedestrian involved in a crash is **over 4 times more likely to die than a motorist (1.32% fatality rate vs. 0.29%)**.
* **Divergence Between Crashes and Fatalities:** While total crash volume **dropped 58.3% between 2018 and 2023**, annual fatalities **increased 15.6% (peaking at 296 in 2021)**. Consequently, the fatality rate per 1,000 crashes nearly **tripled from 1.00 in 2018 to 2.78 in 2022**.
* **Severity Drivers:** Higher vehicle speeds on open roads post-2020, an increase in heavy SUVs/trucks, and a reduction in reported low-speed fender-benders explain why crashes became significantly more lethal.

<br>

**Vehicle Types and Crashes Highlights**
* **Dominance of Personal Passenger Vehicles:** Sedans (48.6%) and SUVs/Station Wagons (37.4%) account for 86% of total crashes (859,318 incidents) and 84.9% of total injuries (312,299).
* **Lethality of Passenger Vehicles:** Standard passenger cars cause nearly two-thirds (64.5%) of all traffic fatalities (Sedans: 626 deaths; SUVs/Station Wagons: 542 deaths).
* **Disproportionate Risk of Motorcycles:** Motorcycles account for 10.5% of all fatalities (190 deaths) despite representing less than 1% of total crash volume.
* **Commercial and Service Impact:** Heavy commercial trucks, box trucks, pickup trucks, and taxis contribute to over 47,000 crashes and secondary casualty risks due to blind spots, double parking, and loading zone congestion
